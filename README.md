## 📘 Project Overview

This project focuses on exploring and classifying biological databases that form the backbone of **genomics**, **proteomics**, and **systems biology** studies.

The analysis covers:
- Structure and purpose of **Primary**, **Secondary**, and **Specialized** databases.  
- Step-by-step dataset retrieval from **GenBank (NCBI)**, **UniProt**, and **KEGG**.  
- Comparative analysis of **sequence data organization** between **NCBI** and **UniProt**.  
- Practical insights into using public databases for biological data interpretation.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Classify databases into **Primary**, **Secondary**, and **Specialized** categories.  
- Identify their **data types**, **formats**, and **research purposes**.  
- Demonstrate how to **retrieve and interpret biological datasets**.  
- Compare **sequence data structure** across major repositories.  
- Highlight the **applications** of database integration in modern bioinformatics.

## 🧩 Database Classification

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|--------------|----------|
| **Primary Database** | Contains raw, experimentally derived data such as nucleotide or protein sequences. | **GenBank (NCBI)** |
| **Secondary Database** | Derived from primary databases with curated, annotated, or interpreted data. | **UniProt** |
| **Specialized Database** | Focused on specific organisms, data types, or biological processes. | **KEGG** |

---

## 🔬 1. Primary Database — **GenBank (NCBI)**

**Data Type:**  
- Nucleotide sequences, annotations, bibliographic information.

**Format:**  
- Flat file, FASTA format.

**Purpose:**  
- To serve as a global repository for **DNA and RNA sequences**, encouraging open scientific access and collaboration.

**Example Dataset:**  
- Search: `TP53 (Tumor Protein 53, Homo sapiens)`  
- Access gene summary, genomic content, expression data, and full FASTA sequence.

**Applications:**  
- Gene discovery  
- Mutation and variant analysis  
- Comparative genomics  
- Development of personalized medicine  

**How to Retrieve:**
1. Visit [NCBI GenBank](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/).  
2. Select **Gene** from the dropdown.  
3. Enter your query (e.g., “TP53 Homo sapiens”).  
4. View the summary, sequence, and related literature.

---

## 🧫 2. Secondary Database — **UniProt**

**Data Type:**  
- Protein sequences, structure, and functional annotations.

**Formats:**  
- Flat text, FASTA, XML, RDF, GFF.

**Purpose:**  
- To provide a **centralized, curated, and annotated protein knowledgebase**.

**Example Dataset:**  
- Search: `HBB (Hemoglobin subunit Beta, Homo sapiens)`  
- Explore protein sequence, domains, variants, and post-translational modifications (PTMs).

**Applications:**  
- Protein structure–function studies  
- Disease variant interpretation  
- Biomarker and drug target discovery  
- Comparative proteomics  

**How to Retrieve:**
1. Visit [UniProt](https://www.uniprot.org/).  
2. Enter protein name or gene ID (e.g., “HBB”).  
3. Explore the “Function,” “Structure,” “Variants,” and “PTM” sections.  
4. Download FASTA or XML format for analysis.

---

## 🧠 3. Specialized Database — **KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes)**

**Data Type:**  
- Genes, proteins, pathways, diseases, drugs, and metabolic networks.

**Format:**  
- KGML (KEGG Markup Language).

**Purpose:**  
- To represent and analyze **biological pathways** and **molecular interactions** for understanding system-level functions.

**Example Dataset:**  
- Search: `CFTR (Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane Conductance Regulator)`  
- View **cAMP signaling pathway** to understand how CFTR influences molecular signaling.

**Applications:**  
- Pathway visualization and annotation  
- Drug target mapping  
- Functional genomics and systems biology  
- Integration of omics data  

**How to Retrieve:**
1. Visit [KEGG](https://www.kegg.jp/).  
2. Enter gene or pathway name (e.g., “CFTR”).  
3. Select the relevant pathway (e.g., cAMP signaling).  
4. Explore the pathway map and reference literature.

## ⚖️ Comparison — **NCBI vs UniProt**

| Feature | **NCBI** | **UniProt** |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Focus** | Nucleotide sequences (DNA/RNA) | Protein sequences and functions |
| **Data Examples** | GenBank, RefSeq, SRA | UniProtKB, UniRef, UniParc |
| **Format** | FASTA, GenBank | ASCII, FASTA |
| **Content** | Raw genomic and transcriptomic data | Curated protein annotations |
| **Applications** | Genomics, variant discovery | Proteomics, functional annotation |
| **Unique ID** | GI number or accession | UniProt accession number |
| **Special Features** | Sequence alignment tools, BLAST | GO annotations, protein domains |

## 🧠 Key Takeaways

- **Primary databases** store raw sequence data.  
- **Secondary databases** curate and annotate biological information.  
- **Specialized databases** integrate and visualize biological systems.  
- Integration of multiple databases enables **multi-omics** and **systems-level** understanding of biology.
